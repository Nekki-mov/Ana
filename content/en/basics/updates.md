# Updates

Every now and then, your system will have updates waiting.

That's completely normal.

In fact…

that's exactly what you want.

---

## Why update at all?

Most updates usually mean one of three things:

* A bug got fixed.
* Something became faster or more stable.
* A security issue got patched.

None of that sounds particularly exciting.

But all of it matters.

More than you might think.

---

## The easy way — Mochi

Open Mochi and head to the **Updates** section in the sidebar.

If anything is waiting, you'll see a list and one very simple button: **Update All**.

Click it.

At this point, the system may ask for your password.

That's completely normal.

It's simply confirming that you're allowing changes to be made to the system.

After that…

all you need to do is wait a moment.

While everything updates, you'll be able to see exactly what's happening.

No frozen screens.

No wondering whether something stopped working.

Just the system doing its job.

Once it finishes…

the update is done.

Although there is one small exception.

If the update includes important system components — like the kernel, for example — restarting is a good idea.

Sometimes, it may even be necessary.

But don't overthink it.

If you need to reboot…

just reboot.

That's all.

---

## The terminal way — dango

If you prefer using the terminal, the process is just as simple.

```
dango -Syu
```

That's it.

One line.

Just like with Mochi, the system may ask for your password before continuing.

Completely normal.

dango will check the official repositories, the AUR, and update everything together.

No extra steps.

No unnecessary complexity.

---

## How often should you update?

There's no strict rule.

But checking every few days is usually a good habit.

Wagashi Linux won't chase you about it.

No red icons demanding attention.

No popups interrupting whatever you were doing.

It'll simply be there.

Waiting.

Whenever you decide.

---

## What if something breaks after an update?

It can happen.

It usually doesn't.

But it can happen.

That's simply part of using a rolling release system like Arch Linux.

If something feels off after updating, you'll find a dedicated chapter for that in the troubleshooting guide.

But keep this in mind:

it's almost never as bad as it feels at first.

---

## One last thing

Updating isn't something you should be afraid of.

It's not a big event.

It's not something you need to overplan.

It's simply part of using your system.

You click a button.

You let it do its job.

And if a reboot is needed…

well, that's a few seconds of your life.

Nothing terrible.

Simple as that.
